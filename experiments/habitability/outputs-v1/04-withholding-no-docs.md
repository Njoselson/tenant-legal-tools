## Elements

1. **A condition affecting habitability, health, safety, or intended use** — RPL § 235-b(1) (three covenants: fit for habitation, fit for intended uses, no conditions dangerous/hazardous/detrimental to life, health or safety). Solow v. Wellner confirms the three distinct covenants and that the protection reaches health/safety/functional habitability, not aesthetics.
2. **The condition was not caused by the tenant or someone under the tenant's control** — RPL § 235-b(1) exception; HMC § 27-2006.
3. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** — from the evidence discussion of Haskins (notice and failure to timely repair "mattered") and the repair-and-deduct conditions drawn from Jangla Realty (notice, no repair within a reasonable time).
4. **Severity and duration sufficient to constitute breach (not de minimis)** — Park West (fact-finder weighs severity, duration, remedial promptness/effectiveness; trivial violations may not breach); Eastside Exhibition (de minimis principle); Solow.
5. **For heat specifically: the code standard** — HMC § 27-2029 (Oct 1–May 31: 68°F 6 a.m.–10 p.m. when outside below 55°F; 62°F 10 p.m.–6 a.m. regardless of outside temperature); § 27-2028 (central heating required in multiple dwellings).
6. **For pests: the code provision** — HMC § 27-2017.4 (pests; classed immediately hazardous) [secondary; section number unverified].
7. **For the abatement amount: rent charged, dates of breach, severity/scope, landlord's remedial efforts** — Park West method (difference between warranted and actual value, expressed as percentage abatement); Solow; Haskins (40% for 14 months).
8. **Procedural posture / defense in a nonpayment case** — Park West (habitability may be raised as defense or counterclaim in a nonpayment case); RPAPL § 711(2) and the written 14-day rent demand requirement [secondary]; RPAPL § 731/§ 749(3) (payment cures) [secondary].

## How the law applies

- **Condition affecting habitability (Element 1):** The tenant states radiators "barely work" and "always mice." Non-working heat implicates § 27-2029/§ 27-2028 and the health/safety covenant; mice implicate § 27-2017.4 (immediately hazardous per the secondary note). These are functional-habitability conditions, not aesthetic ones, so Solow's aesthetic exclusion does not defeat them on their face. *Helps.* But "barely work" is vague — it is unknown whether temperatures fell below the code thresholds, which matters for Element 5.
- **Not tenant-caused (Element 2):** Nothing in the narrative suggests the tenant caused the heating failure or the mice. The tenant did not state the cause either way. *Neutral/helps by default*, but the record is silent.
- **Notice and failure to repair (Element 3):** This is the weakest element. The tenant "never wrote to him" and "mostly told the super in the hallway." Oral notice to a super may or may not count as notice to the landlord; the materials do not resolve whether hallway notice to a super suffices. The super said "he'd take care of it," which is some acknowledgment, but there is no documentation. Haskins and Jangla both treat notice and failure to timely repair as material. *Hurts significantly.*
- **Severity/duration (Element 4):** "Terrible for a long time" and "always mice" suggest duration, but the tenant gives no start date, no end date, and no specifics on severity or scope (whole apartment vs. one room). Park West requires weighing severity and duration; without dates the abatement period cannot be fixed. *Hurts.*
- **Heat code standard (Element 5):** The tenant did not state indoor temperatures, outside temperatures, or dates. Whether § 27-2029 was violated is unknown. *Hurts / cannot determine.*
- **Pest code provision (Element 6):** "Always mice" supports a § 27-2017.4 claim, but the section number is unverified [secondary], and no inspection or violation record exists. *Helps modestly.*
- **Abatement amount (Element 7):** Rent is $1,750/month — that fact is present and helps convert a percentage to dollars. But dates, scope, and remedial efforts are missing. *Mixed.*
- **Procedural posture (Element 8):** The tenant "just got a notice from the landlord's lawyer" and stopped paying four months ago. This appears to be a pre-suit rent demand or the start of a nonpayment proceeding. Under Park West, habitability is a defense/counterclaim in nonpayment. Under the secondary procedure materials, a defective rent demand is a dismissal ground, and full payment before hearing moots the case. The tenant has not stated what the notice says, so whether it is a proper 14-day written demand is unknown. *Mixed.*

## Likely remedy

The most likely available remedy is **breach of the warranty of habitability raised as a defense and counterclaim in the nonpayment proceeding**, seeking a **rent abatement** (Park West; Solow). Repair-and-deduct (Jangla) is unlikely because the tenant mentioned no repair made or paid for. Constructive eviction (Poyck) is not indicated — the tenant has not stated an intent to vacate.

**Abatement range:** A supportable range is **roughly 10% to 40%**, but the low end is more defensible on these facts. Reasoning:
- Park West affirmed **10%**; Solow upheld **10%** for common-area failures plus **1%–11%** for individual apartment issues. These anchor the low-to-mid range for documented, non-catastrophic conditions.
- Haskins awarded **40% for 14 months**, but on facts of "severe, chronic" problems over a year with documented notice and failure to repair — stronger than this tenant's undocumented, vaguely described conditions.
- The tenant's facts (no written notice, no 311, no photos, no dates, vague severity) push toward the **low end, around 10%**, and possibly lower if the conditions are found de minimis (Eastside; Park West). Without dates, the abatement period itself is uncertain; at most it could run for the four months of withholding, but the tenant has not established when the breach began.
- Because the tenant withheld rent without documentation, the risk is a **judgment for full rent and eviction exposure** if breach is not proven (per the defenses/limits note). Full payment of arrears before the hearing would moot a nonpayment case (RPAPL § 731/§ 749(3) [secondary]), and a defective rent demand could defeat the petition — but the notice's contents are unknown.
