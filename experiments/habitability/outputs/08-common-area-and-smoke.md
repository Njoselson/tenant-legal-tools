# Output for 08-common-area-and-smoke

## Elements

1. **A condition affecting the premises or common areas that renders them unfit for human habitation, unfit for their reasonably intended use, or dangerous/hazardous/detrimental to life, health or safety.** Authority: RPL § 235-b(1) (three covenants); Solow v. Wellner (three distinct covenants; health/safety/functional habitability, not aesthetics); Park West v. Mitchell (duty runs to tenant regardless of cause, subject to strike carve-out).
2. **The condition was not caused by the tenant or someone under the tenant's control.** Authority: RPL § 235-b(1) exception; HMC § 27-2006 (tenant duties do not relieve owner).
3. **The condition is not merely de minimis or aesthetic.** Authority: Park West (trivial violations may not breach); Eastside Exhibition (technical violation without real impact on use is not a substantive breach); Solow (no aesthetic/amenity claims).
4. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** (relevant to breach proof and to repair-and-deduct). Authority: Haskins (notice and failure to timely repair mattered); Jangla Realty (notice, reasonable time, necessary repair, reasonable cost) [secondary, weakest].
5. **For common-area/service failures (elevator, trash, pests): the landlord's nondelegable duty to maintain.** Authority: RPL § 235-b (nondelegable); HMC § 27-2005 (general duty to maintain in good repair and habitable condition); Park West (services diminished by building-employee strike still counted, subject to § 235-b(3)).
6. **For the smoke: the landlord's duty to take reasonable steps to abate a nuisance from another unit.** Authority: Poyck v Bryant.
7. **For heat/hot water-type code claims (if applicable): specific code standards.** Authority: HMC § 27-2029 (heat), § 27-2031 (hot water), § 27-2028 (central heating). (Note: the narrative does not state a heat or hot-water problem.)
8. **For mold/pests as code violations:** HMC § 27-2017.3 (mold) and § 27-2017.4 (pests; pests classed immediately hazardous) [secondary; section numbers unverified].
9. **Remedy/defense framework:** habitability as defense or counterclaim in a nonpayment case; damages measured as difference between warranted and actual value, in practice a percentage abatement. Authority: Park West; Solow. Repair-and-deduct: Jangla Realty [secondary, weakest]. HP proceeding for Order to Correct/penalties: RPAPL §§ 110, 117; CCA § 110(a) [secondary].

## How the law applies

- **Element 1 (unfit/dangerous condition):** Helps the tenant strongly. A seven-month elevator outage in a six-story building, with the tenant on the sixth floor, age 71, using a cane, carrying groceries and curtailing outings, plausibly implicates functional habitability and safety under RPL § 235-b(1) and Solow. Overflowing trash and roaches in the hallway implicate health/safety and common areas (Park West; HMC § 27-2005; § 27-2017.4 [secondary]). Secondhand smoke entering the bedroom and worsening asthma maps directly onto Poyck (smoke as breach; landlord duty to take reasonable steps). Hurts: the narrative does not state the size/configuration of the apartment, how often the tenant must use stairs, or medical documentation of the asthma flare; and it does not state whether the smoke is constant or intermittent.
- **Element 2 (not tenant-caused):** Helps: nothing in the narrative suggests the tenant caused the elevator outage, trash overflow, roaches, or neighbor's smoke. Hurts: unknown whether the tenant's own unit conditions contribute to roaches; the narrative does not say.
- **Element 3 (not de minimis/aesthetic):** Helps: elevator outage, pests, and smoke with health impact are functional/health conditions, not aesthetic (Solow). Hurts: the narrative gives no detail on the roach/trash severity or frequency, which could be argued de minimis absent evidence (Park West; Eastside).
- **Element 4 (notice and failure to repair):** Hurts significantly. The tenant told management "in person several times" but has **nothing in writing**. Haskins and Jangla both emphasize notice; oral notice is not per se insufficient, but it is hard to prove. The two HPD violations online are some corroboration that conditions existed and were recorded, but the narrative does not say whether the landlord was served or whether the violations remain open.
- **Element 5 (common-area/service duty):** Helps: elevator, trash room, and hallway are common areas; the duty is nondelegable (RPL § 235-b; HMC § 27-2005; Park West). The strike carve-out (§ 235-b(3)) does not appear to apply on these facts.
- **Element 6 (smoke nuisance):** Helps: Poyck supports a breach and a duty to take reasonable steps against a neighbor's smoke. Hurts: the narrative does not state what, if anything, management said or did in response, nor whether the tenant complained specifically about the smoke (only "several times" generally).
- **Element 7 (heat/hot water):** Not applicable on the stated facts; the tenant did not mention heat or hot water.
- **Element 8 (mold/pests code):** Partially helps: roaches in the hallway may fall under § 27-2017.4 [secondary, unverified]. No mold is mentioned.
- **Element 9 (remedy):** The tenant can raise habitability as a defense/counterclaim in any nonpayment case (Park West) and could seek an HP Order to Correct (RPAPL §§ 110, 117; CCA § 110(a) [secondary]). Repair-and-deduct (Jangla) is weak here because the tenant has not mentioned making or paying for any repair.

## Likely remedy

The most likely remedy is a **rent abatement as a defense/counterclaim** under RPL § 235-b (Park West; Solow), potentially combined with an **HP Order to Correct** (RPAPL §§ 110, 117; CCA § 110(a) [secondary]). Repair-and-deduct (Jangla) is unlikely to be available because no self-repair is described.

**Abatement range:** The materials support a range of roughly **1% to 40%**, but the facts here do not support the top of that range. Reasoning:
- **Elevator outage (seven months, sixth floor, elderly cane user):** This is a serious, prolonged common-area/service failure affecting functional habitability and safety. Park West affirmed 10% for building-wide service loss; Solow upheld 10% for common-area failures plus 1–11% for individual apartment issues. A seven-month outage with a mobility-impaired tenant is at least comparable to Park West's 10% and could justify more, but the materials give no case above 10% for a single service failure.
- **Trash overflow and hallway roaches:** These support an additional increment (Solow's 1–11% per-issue range), but severity and duration are unstated.
- **Smoke/asthma:** Poyck supports breach but the ground truth records **no abatement amount**, so it does not anchor a percentage.
- **Haskins (40% for 14 months)** involved over a year of severe, chronic, multiple hazardous conditions with documented notice and failure to repair. This tenant's facts are less severe and, critically, notice is unwritten and undocumented. Haskins therefore supports the upper bound only if the tenant can prove chronic severity and notice.
- **Realistic range on stated facts:** approximately **10%–20%** overall, with the elevator alone supporting around 10% (Park West/Solow) and the pests/smoke adding increments within Solow's 1–11% per-issue band. The 40% Haskins figure is not supportable without documented notice and proof of chronic severity across multiple conditions. If notice and severity are proven, the range could move toward the middle-to-upper teens; if not, closer to 10% or lower.
