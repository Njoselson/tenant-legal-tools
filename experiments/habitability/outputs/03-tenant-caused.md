# Output for 03-tenant-caused

## Elements

1. **A condition affecting habitability, intended use, or health/safety** — RPL § 235-b(1) (three covenants: fit for human habitation; fit for uses reasonably intended; no conditions dangerous, hazardous, or detrimental to life, health or safety). Solow v. Wellner confirms the three covenants are distinct and that the protection reaches health, safety, and functional habitability, not aesthetics.
2. **The condition was not caused by the tenant or someone under the tenant's control** — RPL § 235-b(1) exception. HMC § 27-2006 (tenant duties do not relieve owner obligations) and § 27-2007 (prohibited tenant acts) are the code-side analogues.
3. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** — Park West (duty runs to tenant; fact-finder weighs remedial promptness and effectiveness); Haskins (notice and failure to timely repair were evidence that mattered); Jangla Realty (notice, no repair within a reasonable time, necessary repair, reasonable cost — for repair-and-deduct).
4. **Severity and duration sufficient to support breach (not de minimis)** — Park West (weighs severity, duration, remedial promptness; de minimis violations may not breach); Eastside Exhibition (not every technical violation is a substantive breach); Solow (aesthetic/amenity complaints excluded).
5. **For any abatement: the rent charged and the period of breach** — Park West (difference between warranted and actual value, in practice a percentage abatement); Solow (percentage methodology); Haskins (40% for 14 months).
6. **For repair-and-deduct: a necessary repair, reasonable cost, and kept receipts/bids/communications** — Jangla Realty (persuasive only).
7. **Procedural posture for withholding rent** — RPAPL § 711(2) and the written 14-day rent demand (HSTPA 2019); RPAPL § 731/§ 749(3) (payment cures); Park West (habitability may be raised as defense or counterclaim in a nonpayment case).

## How the law applies

- **Element 1 (habitability-affecting condition):** Helps the tenant. A leaking toilet wetting the floor for two weeks, a softening floor, and a smell are conditions touching health, safety, and functional habitability (mold/water damage and structural softness are not merely aesthetic under Solow). The hole in the wall is weaker standing alone — a hole is closer to a repair/aesthetic issue unless it exposes wiring, plumbing, or the elements, which the tenant did not state.
- **Element 2 (tenant causation):** Hurts the tenant significantly. The tenant admits her boyfriend punched the wall and kicked/cracked the toilet tank. Under RPL § 235-b(1)'s exception, the landlord's warranty does not reach conditions caused by the tenant or someone under the tenant's control. The boyfriend's acts are the tenant's side of the causation question. The counter-argument in the materials: HMC § 27-2006 says tenant duties do not relieve the owner's obligations, and Park West says the duty is nondelegable — but neither authority in the materials squarely overrides the § 235-b(1) tenant-causation exception for damage the tenant's household caused. The softening floor and smell may be *consequential* damage from the unrepaired leak; whether that consequence is attributed to the tenant's original break or to the landlord's failure to respond after notice is the central contested issue, and the materials do not resolve it.
- **Element 3 (notice and failure to repair):** Helps the tenant. She states she told the landlord about the leak after it started and he has sent no one. Two weeks without response supports "reasonable time" arguments, especially for an active water leak. Jangla's 18-hour lock repair is a much shorter window, but it shows courts treat prompt-response expectations seriously; two weeks of inaction on an active leak is a stronger failure-to-act fact than Jangla's.
- **Element 4 (severity/duration, not de minimis):** Mixed. Two weeks is short relative to Haskins (over a year) and even Park West. Severity is moderate: an active leak, soft floor, and smell are more than trivial, but the tenant describes no health impact, no HPD violations, and no loss of use of a room. Under Park West and Eastside, a short-duration, moderate condition may support a modest abatement or none.
- **Element 5 (abatement math):** Rent is $1,600, which lets a percentage be converted to dollars. Duration is stated as two weeks (so far). Severity is confined to the bathroom (one room), not the whole apartment.
- **Element 6 (repair-and-deduct):** The tenant mentions no repair she made, no cost, no receipts. Jangla's conditions are not shown to be met.
- **Element 7 (withholding rent):** The tenant asks whether she can stop paying. The materials say withholding rent without documentation does not prevent a nonpayment suit; she must then prove breach and abatement, and failing risks judgment for full rent and eviction exposure. A written 14-day rent demand is required (HSTPA 2019), and an overstated demand is a dismissal ground — but nothing in the narrative says a demand was served or what it said. Payment in full before the hearing moots a nonpayment case (RPAPL § 731/§ 749(3)).

## Likely remedy

The most supportable remedy is a **habitability defense/counterclaim in any nonpayment proceeding, seeking a modest rent abatement for the period of the unrepaired leak after notice** — not a rent strike. The materials do not support stopping rent outright: withholding without documentation invites a nonpayment suit, and the tenant would bear the burden of proving breach and abatement (Park West; RPAPL § 711(2)).

**Abatement range:** The materials support, at most, a **low single-digit to roughly 10% abatement for the two-week period**, and even that is contestable. Reasoning:
- Park West affirmed 10% for building-wide loss of doorman, elevator, and garbage services; Solow used 10% for common areas plus 1–11% for individual apartment issues. A single-room, two-week leak with no health impact and no violations is at or below the low end of that band.
- Haskins's 40% is not comparable: it involved over a year of chronic, severe conditions and hazardous violations.
- The tenant-causation exception (RPL § 235-b(1)) may defeat the claim entirely as to the wall hole and cracked tank, and may reduce or eliminate abatement for the leak's consequences if the landlord persuades the court the soft floor and smell flow from the tenant-caused break rather than from the landlord's post-notice inaction.
- Because the tenant admits causing the underlying damage, a court could find **no abatement** and instead find the tenant liable for repairs. The materials do not supply a case allocating causation between tenant-caused damage and landlord failure to mitigate after notice, so the range is genuinely uncertain; I would present it as **0% to ~10%, most likely at the low end**, for the post-notice period only.

Repair-and-deduct is **not** supportable on these facts as stated: no repair was made, no cost or receipts are mentioned, and Jangla's conditions are unmet.
