# Output for 04-withholding-no-docs

## Elements

1. **Existence of the warranty of habitability in the tenancy.** RPL § 235-b(1) — implied in every residential lease, written or oral. [repo + secondary]
2. **A condition that breaches the warranty.** RPL § 235-b(1): premises not fit for human habitation, not fit for uses reasonably intended, or conditions dangerous/hazardous/detrimental to life, health or safety. Park West test: whether, "in the eyes of a reasonable person, defects … deprive the tenant of those essential functions which a residence is expected to provide." Park West, 47 N.Y.2d 316. Insufficient heat and rodent infestation are named examples of qualifying conditions in Park West.
3. **The condition is not de minimis / not merely aesthetic.** Park West (violation may be "de minimis or ha[ve] no impact upon habitability"); Solow v. Wellner, 86 N.Y.2d 582 (objective minimum standard tied to essential residential functions; not lease-based "reasonable expectations").
4. **The condition was not caused by the tenant.** RPL § 235-b(1) exception ("caused by the misconduct of the tenant or lessee or persons under his direction or control"); HMC § 27-2006.
5. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** — part of what mattered in Haskins (100 W 174 LLC v Haskins, 2014 NY Slip Op 51673(U)) and required for repair-and-deduct under Jangla Realty Co. v Gravagna, 112 Misc. 2d 642 (Civ. Ct. 1981). Note: Park West "does not discuss tenant notice as an element."
6. **Duration and severity of the breach, and the landlord's remedial efforts** — the fact-finder "must weigh the severity of the violation and duration of the conditions … as well as the effectiveness of steps taken by the landlord to abate." Park West.
7. **Damages measure:** difference between fair market value as warranted (measured by rent reserved) and value during breach; percentage reduction of rent as setoff in a nonpayment proceeding where tenant counterclaims or pleads breach as a defense. Park West. No expert testimony required. RPL § 235-b(3).
8. **Procedural posture / defenses:** nonpayment proceeding requires a proper written 14-day rent demand that is a good-faith, reasonably accurate accounting; an overstated demand is a dismissal ground. RPAPL § 711(2), § 735 (HSTPA 2019). Payment in full before the hearing moots the case; after judgment but before eviction voids the warrant absent bad-faith withholding. RPAPL § 731 / § 749(3). [secondary]

## How the law applies

- **Element 1 (warranty exists):** Helps. The narrative describes an apartment tenancy; RPL § 235-b applies to every residential lease. No facts suggest a commercial lease (Eastside Exhibition is therefore inapplicable).
- **Element 2 (qualifying condition):** Mixed. "Radiators barely work" implicates insufficient heat, a condition Park West names as habitability-affecting, and HMC § 27-2029 sets specific heat standards (68°F daytime / 62°F nighttime, Oct 1–May 31). "Always mice" implicates rodent infestation, also named in Park West. But the narrative gives no temperatures, no season, no frequency or extent of mice, and no effect on health or use — so whether these rise to "essential functions" deprivation is unestablished.
- **Element 3 (not de minimis):** Hurts. "Barely work" and "always mice" are conclusory; without specifics a court could find the conditions de minimis or aesthetic (Solow, Park West). Eastside is a commercial partial-eviction case and does not supply the residential de minimis standard.
- **Element 4 (not tenant-caused):** Unknown. Nothing in the narrative addresses cause; the tenant did not state whether the mice or radiator problems are attributable to anything the tenant did. HMC § 27-2007 (prohibited tenant acts) is not implicated on these facts.
- **Element 5 (notice and failure to repair):** Hurts significantly. The tenant "never wrote to him" and "mostly told the super in the hallway." Oral notice to a superintendent may count (Jangla found notice to the super sufficient for repair-and-deduct), but the tenant has no documentation and the super's "he'd take care of it" is the only response. Haskins treated notice and failure to repair as evidence that mattered; here notice is undocumented and the landlord's non-response is unproven.
- **Element 6 (duration/severity/landlord efforts):** Mixed. "For a long time" and "always" suggest duration but are imprecise; the tenant has "don't have a lot written down." No evidence of the landlord's remedial efforts either way.
- **Element 7 (damages):** Partially supported. Rent is $1,750/month, so a percentage can be converted to dollars. But the breach period is undefined ("four months" of withholding is not necessarily the breach period), and severity is unquantified.
- **Element 8 (procedure):** The tenant "just got a notice from the landlord's lawyer" — likely a rent demand, but the narrative does not say whether it is a 14-day written demand, whether it is accurate, or whether a petition has been filed. If the demand overstates arrears, that is a dismissal ground; if accurate, the tenant must plead breach as a defense/counterclaim or risk judgment for full rent and eviction exposure.

## Likely remedy

The most likely available remedy is a **breach-of-warranty defense and counterclaim in the nonpayment proceeding, seeking a percentage rent abatement as a setoff** (Park West), plus, if the demand is defective or overstated, **dismissal of the proceeding** (RPAPL § 711(2), § 735 [secondary]). Repair-and-deduct (Jangla) is theoretically available only if the tenant made repairs and can show emergency, notice, reasonable opportunity, and reasonable cost — the narrative does not say the tenant repaired anything.

**Abatement range:** The materials support a range of roughly **1% to 40%**, but on these facts the supportable range is at the low end — approximately **1% to 10%** — with the caveat that no specific percentage is firmly supportable because severity and duration are unquantified. Reasoning:
- Park West affirmed a **10%** reduction for 17 days of severe, stipulated conditions (trash to first-floor windows, health emergency, rats/roaches/vermin) with "feeble attempts" by the landlord to alleviate. That is the high anchor for documented, severe, short-duration conditions.
- Solow involved elevator problems and produced percentages of 10% (common areas) and 1–11% (individual apartments) at the trial level, but the Court of Appeals **did not endorse any percentage** — those figures were left for recalculation on remand. They are therefore weak anchors.
- Haskins awarded **40% for 14 months** for severe, chronic, documented conditions (persistent rodent infestation, broken facilities, hazardous violations, notice, failure to repair). That is the high end and depends on documentation and duration the tenant lacks.
- Here the tenant has **no written notice, no 311/HPD record, no photos, and only vague duration** ("a long time," "always"). Under Park West the fact-finder weighs severity, duration, and the landlord's efforts; the tenant can prove little of any. A court could find the conditions de minimis (Solow, Park West) and award **0%**. If the tenant's testimony establishes persistent mice and inadequate heat over a meaningful period, a modest abatement in the **1%–10%** range is the most that the materials' anchors support. The 40% Haskins figure is not supportable without the documentation and duration that case relied on.
