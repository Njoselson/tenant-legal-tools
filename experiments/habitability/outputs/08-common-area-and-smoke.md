# Output for 08-common-area-and-smoke

## Elements

1. **Existence of the warranty of habitability in the tenant's lease.** RPL § 235-b(1) — implied in every residential lease, written or oral. [repo + secondary]
2. **A condition that breaches one of the three statutory covenants:** (a) premises/common areas not fit for human habitation; (b) not fit for uses reasonably intended; (c) occupants subjected to conditions dangerous, hazardous, or detrimental to life, health, or safety. RPL § 235-b(1); Park West, 47 N.Y.2d 316. [opinion]
3. **The condition materially affects health, safety, or essential residential functions** — not merely aesthetic or de minimis. Park West ("materially affect the health and safety"; reasonable-person test); Solow v. Wellner, 86 N.Y.2d 582 (objective minimum standard tied to essential residential functions; operable elevator essential in a high-rise). [opinion]
4. **The condition is not caused by the tenant's own misconduct.** RPL § 235-b(1) exception (quoted in Park West, 47 N.Y.2d at 325 n.1); HMC § 27-2006. [opinion/repo]
5. **Notice to the landlord and a failure to repair within a reasonable time** — part of what mattered in Haskins and Poyck; Jangla requires notice for repair-and-deduct. [repo/opinion]
6. **Duration and severity of the breach, and the landlord's remedial efforts, to fix the abatement amount.** Park West (fact-finder "must weigh the severity of the violation and duration of the conditions ... as well as the effectiveness of steps taken by the landlord to abate"). [opinion]
7. **Damages measured as difference between value as warranted (rent reserved) and value during breach**, awarded as a percentage rent reduction/setoff in a nonpayment proceeding. Park West. [opinion]
8. **No expert testimony required** to establish damages. RPL § 235-b(3) (as quoted in Park West). [opinion]

## How the law applies

- **Element 1 (warranty exists):** Helps. The narrative describes a residential apartment with rent of $1,500; the warranty is implied regardless of lease form. No facts hurt this element.
- **Element 2 (breach of a covenant):** Helps strongly on the elevator. Solow holds operable elevator service is essential in a high-rise; a six-story building with a seven-month outage and a 71-year-old cane user on the sixth floor fits the "essential functions" and "health/safety" covenants. Trash overflow and hallway roaches fit Park West's examples (inadequate sanitation, insect/rodent infestation). Secondhand smoke fits Poyck (smoke through vents causing health problems is a breach; landlord has a duty to take reasonable steps to abate). Hurts: the narrative does not state the building is a multiple dwelling or high-rise beyond "six-story," and does not describe the extent of the trash/roach problem (one hallway? whole floor?).
- **Element 3 (material, not de minimis):** Helps on the elevator (seven months, mobility impairment, curtailed going out) and on smoke (asthma aggravation). Hurts on trash/roaches: the narrative gives no scale, duration, or health impact, so a court could treat these as lesser or de minimis under Park West/Solow. Eastside is a commercial partial-eviction case and is not authority here.
- **Element 4 (no tenant misconduct):** Neutral/unknown. Nothing in the narrative suggests the tenant caused the elevator outage, trash, roaches, or smoke. But the smoke originates from another unit — Poyck shows a landlord still has a duty to take reasonable steps; the statutory misconduct exception concerns the tenant's own or directed persons' conduct, which is not implicated on these facts.
- **Element 5 (notice and failure to repair):** Mixed. Helps: tenant told management in person several times; HPD violations exist online, which implies a 311 complaint and inspection. Hurts badly: "nothing in writing." Jangla and the evidence notes treat dated written complaints as the strong proof; oral notice is provable only by the tenant's testimony and is contestable. The narrative does not say what management said or did in response, or whether repairs were attempted.
- **Element 6 (duration/severity/landlord efforts):** Helps on duration for the elevator (seven months). Hurts: no start/end dates for trash, roaches, or smoke; no information on landlord remedial efforts for any condition.
- **Element 7 (damages):** Helps: rent is $1,500, so a percentage can be converted to dollars. Hurts: no statement of the period over which rent was withheld or sought to be abated, and no breakdown of which conditions affected which parts of the premises.
- **Element 8 (no expert needed):** Helps. The tenant's own testimony about the elevator, carrying groceries, curtailed outings, and asthma can support the breach and amount without an expert (Park West; Poyck).

## Likely remedy

The most likely remedy is a **breach-of-warranty defense and rent-abatement setoff** in any nonpayment proceeding (Park West), and/or an **HP proceeding** seeking an Order to Correct (RPAPL §§ 110, 117; CCA § 110(a)). Repair-and-deduct (Jangla) is unlikely on these facts: the elevator is a common element the tenant cannot self-repair, and the narrative shows no refusal after reasonable opportunity in the Jangla sense.

**Abatement range:** A supportable range is roughly **10%–25%**, with the elevator outage as the anchor. Reasoning from the materials:
- Park West affirmed **10%** for 17 days of severe, building-wide conditions (trash, rats, no extermination) — a short but extreme breach.
- Solow left percentages to be recalculated but the Appellate Term had allowed **5%** common-area abatement for elevator problems; the Court of Appeals endorsed that operable elevator service is essential in a high-rise without endorsing a number.
- Haskins awarded **40% for 14 months** for chronic rodent infestation plus multiple broken facilities and hazardous violations with notice and failure to repair — the closest analogue for duration, but Haskins involved more conditions and documented notice.
- Here the elevator outage is long (seven months) and hits an elderly cane user on the sixth floor, which pushes above the Solow 5% elevator figure; but notice is oral only, the trash/roach and smoke conditions are undocumented as to duration and scale, and there is no evidence of landlord efforts either way. That supports a mid-range figure rather than the Haskins 40%. The smoke (Poyck) and trash/roaches could add incrementally if documented, but Poyck granted no amount and the trash/roach facts are thin.

The range is not precise because the materials give no formula; it is reasoned by analogy to the 5%–40% span in the cases, weighted toward the elevator's duration and the tenant's vulnerability, discounted for the notice and documentation gaps.
