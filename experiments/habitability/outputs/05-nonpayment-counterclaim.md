# Output for 05-nonpayment-counterclaim

## Elements

1. **Existence of the warranty of habitability in the tenancy.** RPL § 235-b(1) — implied in every residential lease, written or oral. [repo + secondary]
2. **A condition that breaches the warranty** — premises not fit for human habitation, not fit for uses reasonably intended, or conditions dangerous/hazardous/detrimental to life, health or safety. RPL § 235-b(1); Park West, 47 N.Y.2d 316 (test: whether, "in the eyes of a reasonable person, defects ... deprive the tenant of those essential functions which a residence is expected to provide"). [opinion]
3. **The condition is not caused by the tenant's own misconduct.** RPL § 235-b(1) exception (quoted in Park West, 47 N.Y.2d at 325 n.1); HMC § 27-2006. [opinion/repo]
4. **The condition is not de minimis or merely aesthetic.** Park West (a code violation may be "de minimis or ha[ve] no impact upon habitability"; parties must then produce evidence on extent, impact, and landlord's measures); Solow v. Wellner, 86 N.Y.2d 582 (objective minimum standard tied to essential residential functions). [opinion]
5. **A housing-code violation as prima facie evidence of breach** (not required, not dispositive). Park West; HMC §§ 27-2005, 27-2017.3 (mold), 27-2017.4 (pests) [secondary, section numbers unverified]; violation classes (Class B = hazardous, 30 days) [secondary].
6. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** — part of what mattered in Haskins, 2014 NY Slip Op 51673(U); required for repair-and-deduct under Jangla Realty Co. v. Gravagna, 112 Misc. 2d 642 (Civ. Ct. 1981) (persuasive only). Note: Park West "does not discuss tenant notice as an element." [opinion/repo]
7. **Damages measured as difference between value as warranted (measured by rent reserved) and value during breach**, awarded as a percentage rent reduction/setoff in a nonpayment proceeding. Park West; RPL § 235-b(3) (no expert testimony required). [opinion]
8. **Procedural defenses to the nonpayment case:** proper written 14-day rent demand that is a good-faith, reasonably accurate accounting (an overstated demand is a dismissal ground); RPAPL § 711(2), § 735 [secondary]. Payment in full before the hearing moots the case; after judgment but before eviction voids the warrant (absent bad-faith withholding) — RPAPL § 731 / § 749(3) [secondary].

## How the law applies

- **Element 1 (warranty exists):** Helps. The narrative describes a residential tenancy at $1,950/month. Nothing suggests a commercial lease (Eastside Exhibition is therefore inapplicable — it is a commercial actual-partial-eviction case and does not address § 235-b).
- **Element 2 (breach):** Helps strongly. A collapsing bathroom ceiling from an upstairs leak, fallen plaster, and black mold on ceiling and walls are conditions that, under Park West's reasonable-person test, plausibly deprive the tenant of essential residential functions and threaten health/safety. Solow's objective standard is met on these facts if proven.
- **Element 3 (not tenant-caused):** Helps. The narrative attributes the condition to a leak from upstairs — an "act of third part[y]" or ordinary deterioration, which Park West expressly places within the warranty. Nothing in the narrative suggests tenant misconduct. (Unknown facts could hurt; see Cannot determine.)
- **Element 4 (not de minimis):** Helps. Unlike Eastside's 12 square feet of cross-bracing, a collapsing ceiling with mold is not minimal. The open Class B violation supports non-triviality, though Park West requires evidence of extent and impact beyond the violation itself.
- **Element 5 (code violation as prima facie evidence):** Helps. An HPD-inspected, still-open Class B violation for the leak and a mold violation is prima facie evidence of uninhabitability under Park West. It is not dispositive; the landlord may still contest extent and impact.
- **Element 6 (notice and failure to repair):** Helps. Email and certified letter in the first week, with receipts, plus an open violation five months later, supports notice and failure to timely repair (Haskins). Park West does not require notice as an element, so this is strengthening rather than essential.
- **Element 7 (damages):** Mixed. The tenant has the rent figure ($1,950/month) and a five-month duration, which supports a percentage calculation. But the narrative does not state how much of the apartment is affected (whole apartment vs. bathroom only), the landlord's remedial efforts, or health impacts — all factors Park West says the fact-finder must weigh.
- **Element 8 (procedural defenses):** Helps on the demand. The written demand says $4,300 while the tenant says $3,900 is owed; under the [secondary] rule that an overstated demand is a dismissal ground, this discrepancy is a potential defense. Hurts on escrow: the tenant never paid withheld rent into escrow, which does not itself defeat the habitability defense but means the tenant bears the burden of proving breach and abatement and risks judgment for full rent if proof fails. Full payment of arrears before the hearing would moot the case (RPAPL § 731 / § 749(3) [secondary]) — the tenant has not said whether that is possible.

## Likely remedy

The most likely remedy is a **breach-of-warranty defense and rent-abatement setoff in the nonpayment proceeding** (Park West permits breach pleaded as a defense or counterclaim, with a percentage reduction of rent), plus a possible **dismissal of the petition if the $4,300 demand is found overstated** ([secondary] rule; not established from the materials whether the discrepancy is an overstatement versus arrears the tenant has not accounted for).

**Abatement range:** The materials support a range of roughly **10% to 40%**, with the facts pointing toward the middle-to-upper part of that range, but no precise figure is supportable:
- Park West affirmed **10%** for 17 days of severe building-wide conditions (trash, rats, no extermination) — a floor analogy.
- Haskins awarded **40% for 14 months** for chronic rodent infestation, broken facilities, and hazardous violations persisting over a year with notice and failure to repair — the closest factual analogy here (long duration, hazardous conditions, notice, no repair), though the court level is not stated in the repo.
- Solow's percentages (10% common areas; 1–11% individual units; 5% on appeal) were **not endorsed** by the Court of Appeals and were remanded for recalculation; they are weak comparators.
- The tenant's five-month duration and open Class B/mold violations resemble Haskins more than Park West, supporting an abatement above 10%; but the narrative does not establish that the whole apartment is unusable (if only the bathroom is affected, the percentage should be lower), and the landlord's remedial efforts are unknown. A defensible estimate is therefore **roughly 15%–40%**, with the exact figure turning on scope, duration, and the landlord's response. This is an estimate from analogous cases, not a rule; the materials supply no formula.
