# Output for 05-nonpayment-counterclaim

## Elements

1. **Existence of the warranty** — RPL § 235-b(1) is implied in every residential lease, written or oral. [repo + secondary]
2. **Condition rendering the premises unfit for human habitation, unfit for intended use, or dangerous/hazardous/detrimental to life, health or safety** — RPL § 235-b(1); the three covenants are distinct per Solow v. Wellner, 86 N.Y.2d 582 (1995). [secondary]
3. **Condition not caused by the tenant or someone under the tenant's control** — RPL § 235-b(1) exception; HMC § 27-2006. [repo + secondary]
4. **Notice to the landlord and failure to repair within a reasonable time** — Park West Mgmt. Corp. v. Mitchell, 47 N.Y.2d 316 (1979); 100 W 174 LLC v. Haskins, 2014 NY Slip Op 51673(U); Poyck v. Bryant, 2006 NY Slip Op 26343. [secondary / repo]
5. **The condition is not de minimis or merely aesthetic** — Park West; Solow; Eastside Exhibition Corp. v. 210 East 86th St. Corp., 18 N.Y.3d 1035 (2012). [secondary]
6. **Breach may be raised as a defense or counterclaim in a nonpayment proceeding** — Park West. [secondary]
7. **For repair-and-deduct (if pursued): notice, no repair within a reasonable time, a necessary repair (ideally a code violation), reasonable cost, and kept bids/receipts/communications** — Jangla Realty Co. v. Gravagna, 112 Misc. 2d 642 (Civ. Ct. Queens Co. 1981). [secondary, weakest]
8. **For a nonpayment defense on the demand: a good-faith, reasonably accurate written 14-day rent demand; an overstated demand is a dismissal ground** — RPAPL § 711(2), § 735; HSTPA 2019. [secondary]
9. **Payment in full before the hearing moots the case; payment after judgment but before execution voids the warrant absent bad-faith withholding** — RPAPL § 731 / § 749(3). [secondary]
10. **HP proceeding for an Order to Correct, civil penalties, and in severe cases a 7-A Administrator** — RPAPL §§ 110, 117; CCA § 110(a); RPAPL Art. 7-A §§ 769–783. [secondary]

## Likely remedy

The most likely available remedy is a **habitability defense/counterclaim in the nonpayment proceeding** (Park West), seeking a **rent abatement**, plus a possible **dismissal challenge to the overstated rent demand** (RPAPL § 711(2)/§ 735) and an **HP Order to Correct** for the open violations.

**Abatement range:** The materials support a range of roughly **10% to 40%**, with the facts here pointing toward the **middle-to-upper part of that range but not the top**. Reasoning:
- Park West affirmed **10%**; Solow upheld **10%** common-area plus **1%–11%** per-apartment abatements.
- Haskins awarded **40% for 14 months** for over a year of severe, chronic conditions including persistent infestation and multiple hazardous violations.
- Here the conditions are serious (structural collapse, mold, open Class B violation) but the tenant describes them as concentrated in the **bathroom**, over **about five months**, not a year-plus of building-wide chronic failure. That places it above the 10% baseline but below Haskins' 40%.
- A defensible estimate is **approximately 20%–30%** of rent for the roughly five-month breach period, subject to proof of duration, scope, and the landlord's response. This is an estimate, not a figure fixed by any single authority in the materials.

No abatement is supportable for repair-and-deduct because the tenant did not state any repair was made or paid for.
